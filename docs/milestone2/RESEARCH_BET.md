# A more ambitious Recros research bet

October 14, 2026. Presentation: October 15. Status: interactive prototype and matched-observation DeepSeek comparison implemented and run. The first personal trials exposed an unfamiliar-work problem; familiar-work selection and a representation audit now address it. No human improvement is established.

## The hypothesis worth risking failure on

**Can a compact language-model front end plus explicit information planning match or beat a larger language-model recommender, when both receive the same evidence, tools and interaction budget?**

The proposed advantage is knowing what information to obtain. More reasoning over the same incomplete input cannot reveal a particular person's unobserved preference. The system should distinguish a missing user preference from a missing item fact, and recognize when both must be resolved together.

This extends the previous one-question idea substantially: the research object becomes the diagnosis and repair of failed transfer. The central experiment is no longer merely whether an extra question improves a ranking.

No “never researched before” claim is established. Robust elicitation, action planning and tool-using recommendation already exist. Our proposed contribution must be a useful, separately evaluated cross-domain benchmark and method under incomplete evidence, with explicit assumptions and a strong baseline.

## The lead idea in one example

A person likes a film about grief. That observation is compatible with several tastes: they seek emotional catharsis, value the acting, prefer hopeful endings, or want an entirely different mood when listening to music. A proposed song also has incomplete descriptive evidence.

Before recommending, Recros constructs alternative preference/evidence hypotheses consistent with what is known. It searches for one under which its proposed recommendation loses to another candidate. Such a counterexample is a **witness**, not an invented claim about this particular person.

Then it chooses among four actions:

- **Ask the user** when their preference or intended cross-domain relationship matters.
- **Inspect item evidence** when a candidate's relevant attribute is unknown and a permitted source can resolve it.
- **Recommend** when the remaining ambiguity does not change the decision enough to justify further effort.
- **Abstain or offer a broader choice** when the available evidence and budget cannot support the decision.

This is not a pair of LLMs debating until they agree. The prototype uses an explicit finite table of hypotheses and a deterministic checker. In the proposed real system, a language model interprets descriptions and proposes hypotheses; it does not certify their completeness or invent missing facts.

## The interesting failure of one-step thinking

Consider two hypothetical songs, A and B. One has a hopeful resolution and the other an unresolved ending. Both item descriptions omit the ending. The listener's preferred ending is also unknown. A third neutral item has utility 0.5 in every constructed world.

| Possible world | User wants | A's ending | Utility A | Utility B |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | Hopeful | Hopeful | 1 | 0 |
| 2 | Hopeful | Unresolved | 0 | 1 |
| 3 | Unresolved | Hopeful | 0 | 1 |
| 4 | Unresolved | Unresolved | 1 | 0 |

These are deliberately constructed utilities, not judgments about real songs.

Asking what the listener wants alone leaves uncertainty about which song supplies it. Reading A's ending alone leaves uncertainty about whether the listener wants that ending. Doing both identifies the best candidate. The information is complementary: neither action alone reduces the minimum worst-case recommendation regret here.

An information-gain policy may still choose one action because it removes hypotheses. A one-step **decision-value** policy can stop prematurely because its immediate improvement is zero. Our experiment must therefore compare both kinds of policy rather than selecting an artificially weak baseline.

## Formal mechanism and exact limits

Let W be a finite set of possible preference/evidence worlds, I the candidate items, and u(w, i) an explicitly specified utility. The regret of recommending i in w is:

`r(w, i) = max(j in I) u(w, j) - u(w, i)`.

The best deterministic recommendation minimizes `max(w in W) r(w, i)`. An attaining world and competing item provide the witness. We also permit abstention with a specified loss λ.

For an information-action budget h, the exact finite planner chooses between stopping and paying for an action a:

`V(W, h) = min(stop_loss(W), min_a [cost(a) + max_o V(W consistent with o, h-1)])`.

Each action is used at most once. The costs and abstention loss share the utility scale. They are hypothetical in the probe and require sensitivity analysis and empirical justification in any deployed version. The planner uses worst-case outcomes, not an estimated probability distribution.

This is established robust decision theory applied in a small executable model. It is not a new theorem, a bound on real NDCG, or a calibrated probability of being right. It requires the real state to be included among the worlds, correct utility specification, and truthful modeled observations. A novel user answer must invalidate or expand the model, never be forced into an existing category.

## What is implemented today

- [Exact planner](../../apps/api/recros_api/decision.py) and [fixtures](../../ml/src/recros_ml/research/decision_probe.py): separate question/evidence actions, a bounded plan tree, witness extraction, recommendation and abstention.
- [Seven meaningful tests](../../ml/tests/test_decision_probe.py): distinguish the missing information, handle complementary actions and unavailable evidence, exhaustively replay policies, check bounds, and demonstrate failure when the true world is omitted.
- [Generated results](results/DECISION_PROBE.md) and [full plan/replay data](results/decision_probe.json).

The seven original mechanism tests pass; the final expanded suite passes 86 tests. No model inference or download was needed for the mathematical probe. The planner is now integrated into the API; the movie-only ranking pilot remains a separate experiment.

The [decision lab](DECISION_LAB.md) now supports real preference answers, retrieval of cached sourced passages, all six domain directions, outside-model stops, local ratings and export. The [policy experiment](results/policy/RESULTS.md) ran 24 scripted tasks against six deterministic policies and DeepSeek V4 Flash through the existing Command Code subscription. Exact planning, information gain and DeepSeek tied: 24/24 recommendations matched the scripted theme, with mean hypothetical loss 0.030 and 0.5 questions plus 0.5 evidence accesses per task. The one-step policy recommended on 18/24 tasks and abstained on the six joint-uncertainty cases.

**This does not establish an advantage over the strong baselines.** It confirms the mechanism and limits the claim: given our simple structured model, extra LLM planning is unnecessary, and this structured planning comparison does not measure language extraction or real preference benefit.
A separate tiny-model comparison and human reading pilot are now complete; see below. The same 12 works are reused; the 24 tasks are not independent human observations. The protocol and prompt were frozen locally before inference, and all final responses and usage are retained.

In the four-world example, the one-step planner abstains at hypothetical loss 0.45. With two actions it pays 0.04 for a preference answer and 0.02 for an item fact, then recommends with zero terminal regret within the declared worlds. Total loss is 0.06. **Do not report this as an 87% real-world improvement.** The values follow from a constructed problem and selected costs.

The deliberately omitted-world test is equally important: a zero-regret certificate on an incomplete model can hide regret 1 in a missing world. Thus the most difficult research component is building and validating the semantic uncertainty model, not solving the tiny table.

The proposed research intersection is **independently varied user ambiguity and item-evidence absence, complementary information actions, and measured cross-domain usefulness under a shared action budget**. The targeted search did not establish that this exact setup is new. Before claiming novelty, trace the references/citations of the closest works and reproduce an appropriate baseline.

## The experiment that could make this substantial

### A. Diagnose the failure, not just the final ranking

Construct a factorial benchmark with four conditions: preferences known/ambiguous × item facts available/missing. Include valid no-transfer preferences, failed evidence retrieval, “none of these” responses, incorrect facet extraction, and ambiguities that do not affect the best item. Controlled deletions must have a retained source of truth; never invent new facts to resolve them.

Use real catalog items and independent human judgments where possible. Keep constructed cases in a separate mechanism suite. Movies, books and individual songs need their own evidence sources; a Spotify audio feature is not a narrative fact. Report all six transfer directions with actual counts, without inflating sample size through relabeling identical fixtures.

### B. Give every baseline the same opportunity

Compare content ranking, the existing evidence bridge, always-ask, one-step regret reduction, information-gain selection, a two-step exact planner, and direct DeepSeek with the **same** ask/inspect actions and budget. Record all prompts and tool observations. A larger model denied the decisive evidence is not a fair baseline for a smaller model allowed to retrieve it.

Separate language interpretation from planning: first give all policies the same verified structured state; then test end-to-end behavior on text. This reveals whether an improvement comes from planning or from a better input representation. Include oracle structured state only as an explicitly privileged upper bound.

### C. Measure what matters

Primary empirical outcome: blinded participant-rated recommendation usefulness at matched user-question budgets. Report evidence accesses, latency and tokens separately rather than inventing a monetary or combined cost score. Secondary outcomes include unnecessary questions, unsupported assertions, abstention/coverage, wrong decisions despite a claimed certificate, and performance under missing-hypothesis perturbations. When abstention changes coverage, report risk-versus-coverage comparisons so refusing everything cannot count as winning.

Use participant-level uncertainty estimates. Freeze prompts and selection rules before the held-out judgments. A tiny pilot is exploratory. Our present synthetic probe cannot establish these outcomes.

### D. Put the SLM where it adds value

A verified small model could extract evidence-backed attributes and candidate interpretations; the inexpensive exact checker can handle a bounded decision state. Do not train a language model to imitate arithmetic that is already cheap and exact. Compare the same checker behind a small and a larger language-model front end, measuring omitted hypotheses and semantic errors as well as cost.

## Scope before tomorrow versus the research ambition

**Ready now:** the recovered application, evidence demo, earlier movie experiment, literature comparison, exact decision probe, interactive decision lab and matched-observation DeepSeek comparison. See [the trial guide](DECISION_LAB.md).

**Implemented slice:** expose two possible target themes and a missing candidate assignment; support a real user answer and retrieval of held-back, attributed catalog passages. Log each observation and decision. The hypotheses are hand specified, not independently validated as an adequate model of users.

**Next research slice:** use the single available participant's qualitative mismatches to improve the hypothesis representation, then obtain independent judgments on a frozen version. Test a verified small-model front end if available. The current matched-tool DeepSeek baseline ties the planner; report that result. Do not claim a completed independent human study or validated SLM by October 15 unless it has actually happened.

**Stop or revise** if the joint-uncertainty cases are uncommon in actual use, if the real hypothesis is often omitted, if users cannot answer reliably, if evidence retrieval seldom resolves the issue, or if the direct model reaches the same quality with lower effort. Those are falsifiable risks, not details to hide.

An ambitious presentation claim that remains honest: “We are studying whether cross-domain recommendation can be improved by identifying and resolving the specific information that makes a transfer unreliable. We have implemented the decision mechanism and exposed its assumptions; preference improvement is the next empirical test.”
